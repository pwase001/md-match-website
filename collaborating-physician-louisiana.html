Louisiana State Guide

Collaborating Physicians in Louisiana: What APRNs Need

Louisiana will not let you start until the Board says so — and it will not accept your own paperwork.

Reviewed by Philip Wasef, MD — actively collaborating physician, licensed in 45 states including Louisiana. Last updated: September 2026.

Two things make Louisiana distinctive. You must obtain written approval from the Louisiana State Board of Nursing for your collaborating physician before clinical practice begins. And your collaborative practice agreement must use the Board's own template — customised forms aren't accepted, however well drafted. Then there's the licensing sequence. Louisiana requires a state Controlled Dangerous Substance licence in addition to your federal DEA registration, obtained in a specific order, with the whole chain to be completed inside 60 days of your LSBN approval. None of this is difficult. All of it is exacting, and the failures I see are almost always sequencing failures rather than substantive ones.

Quick Answers

Do Louisiana APRNs need a collaborating physician?
Yes. APRNs practise under a collaborative practice agreement with one or more physicians, and prescriptive authority requires Board approval.
Can I practise while approval is pending?
No. You need written approval from LSBN for your collaborating physician prior to clinical practice.
Can I use my own agreement template?
No. The CPA must be based on the Board's template. Customised forms are not acceptable.
Is the agreement submitted to the Board?
The CPA itself is retained at the practice site. The Attestation of Collaborative Practice, listing every physician you'll collaborate with, is uploaded during the application.
How many APRNs can one physician collaborate with?
No numeric limit is specified.
Is there a distance requirement?
No mileage rule. But your collaborating physician must be actively engaged in direct patient care in Louisiana, practising in an area comparable in scope, specialty or expertise to yours.
Is chart review required?
No mandated percentage or schedule. The Board does have authority to conduct random audits of patient records, facility policies and related documents.
Do I need a state controlled substance licence?
Yes — you do. Louisiana requires a CDS licence from the Board of Pharmacy in addition to your federal DEA registration, and the state licence comes first.
Does my collaborating physician need one?
No. The CDS and DEA credentials are the prescriber's — yours. LSBN's requirements for the collaborating physician concern their medical licensure and active Louisiana practice, not controlled substance registrations. This is worth knowing, because some states do require it of the physician.
What about changes to my arrangement?
Notify the Board in writing requesting approval of all changes regarding physicians and practice sites, within 30 days. A physician voluntarily ending an agreement must give no less than 30 days' notice.

The Sequence, and the 60-Day Clock

This is where Louisiana arrangements go wrong, and it's entirely avoidable.

Controlled substance authority runs in five steps, in order:

  1. Submit your application to LSBN with the required documentation.
  2. Receive your LSBN approval letter.
  3. Apply for your state CDS licence from the Louisiana Board of Pharmacy.
  4. Apply for your federal DEA registration.
  5. Send copies of both licences back to LSBN within 30 days.
All of it inside 60 days of your LSBN approval.

Two things people get wrong. They apply for the DEA before the state CDS licence — the Board of Pharmacy licence is the prerequisite, and the federal application follows it. And they treat the LSBN approval letter as the finish line rather than the starting gun on a 60-day clock.

One thing worth not worrying about. These credentials are yours, not your physician's. Louisiana doesn't require your collaborating physician to hold a state CDS licence for you to prescribe — their eligibility turns on their medical licence and active Louisiana practice. That matters when you're evaluating a prospective collaborator: it's a question you don't need to ask, and one that rules out fewer physicians here than in states that do impose it.

If you're relocating from a state with no separate registration, this is the step that catches you out. Build the calendar backwards from your intended start date rather than forwards from your application.

Which Controlled Substances You're Asking For

Louisiana doesn't grant controlled substance authority as a single block. You specify which tier you're seeking in your attestation and your CPA:

  • Schedules III through V
  • Schedule II non-narcotic — this is the ADHD tier
  • Full Schedule II narcotics

For psychiatric practice this distinction is the one to get right. Stimulants for ADHD sit in the Schedule II non-narcotic tier. If your attestation and CPA request Schedules III–V only, you cannot prescribe them — and correcting that afterwards means going back through the approval process rather than amending a form.

Decide what your practice actually requires before you file, not after your first patient needs something you didn't ask for.

What the Agreement Must Contain

Louisiana's CPA is template-based, but the template exists to capture specific things. It must be in writing, signed by both you and the physician, and retained at the practice site. Required components include:

  • Availability of the collaborating physician for consultation and referral
  • Methods of management, including clinical practice guidelines
  • Coverage during absences
  • A plan of accountability defining your prescriptive authority
  • Provisions on hospital privileges
  • Procedures for diagnostic and laboratory requests
  • A plan for medical records documentation
  • Acknowledgment of mutual responsibility

Your physician must be actively engaged in the provision of direct patient care in Louisiana, practising in an area comparable in scope, specialty or expertise to yours. That last requirement rules out arrangements with a physician whose practice bears no relation to yours, however willing they are.

Approval Checklist

  1. Confirm your collaborating physician qualifies — actively engaged in direct patient care in Louisiana, comparable in scope, specialty or expertise.
  2. Complete the CPA on the Board's template, signed by both parties, covering all required components including allowed medications, methods for reviewing records, referral, and reviewing patient outcomes. Retain it at the practice site.
  3. Complete the Attestation of Collaborative Practice, listing every physician you'll collaborate with, and upload it with your application.
  4. Submit to LSBN and wait for written approval. Do not begin clinical practice before it arrives.
  5. Apply for your Louisiana CDS licence, then your federal DEA registration — in that order.
  6. Send copies of both to LSBN within 30 days, with the whole sequence complete inside 60 days of approval.
  7. For any change of physician or practice site, notify the Board in writing requesting approval, within 30 days.

What a Collaborating Physician Costs in Louisiana

Louisiana sits mid-range, and the drivers aren't the usual ones.

There's no cap on how many APRNs a physician may collaborate with and no mileage rule, so neither of the constraints that inflate prices in California or Mississippi applies here. There's also no mandated chart review percentage, so the recurring burden on the physician is lighter than in Missouri or Tennessee.

What Louisiana costs you is a narrower pool and a stricter gate. Your physician has to be actively practising in Louisiana in a comparable specialty — which rules out the out-of-state and loosely-matched arrangements that work elsewhere. And the approval gate means weeks between signing and seeing patients.

So the thing to optimise for here is a physician who qualifies cleanly and will move promptly. A collaborator who is technically eligible but slow on paperwork costs you more in delayed earnings than you'll save on rate.

Mistakes I Actually See in Louisiana Arrangements

Practising before written approval arrives.

LSBN requires written approval of your collaborating physician prior to clinical practice. A signed agreement isn't authorisation.

Using a custom agreement.

The CPA must be based on the Board's template. A better-drafted bespoke agreement is still the wrong document.

Applying for the DEA before the state CDS licence.

The Louisiana licence is the prerequisite. Doing it backwards means doing it twice.

Missing the 60-day window.

The LSBN approval letter starts a clock. People treat it as the end of the process.

Requesting the wrong controlled substance tier.

Schedule II non-narcotic is a separate ask from Schedules III–V. If you prescribe stimulants and didn't request that tier, you're not authorised for them.

A physician whose practice isn't comparable.

Willingness isn't enough — the rule requires comparable scope, specialty or expertise, and an out-of-state physician doesn't satisfy the active-Louisiana-practice requirement at all.

Changing physicians without Board approval.

Changes require written notification requesting approval, not just notice.

Forgetting to send the licence copies back.

Step five is easy to drop once you have the licences in hand, and it's within 30 days.
