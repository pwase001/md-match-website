New York State Guide

Collaborating Physicians in New York: What NPs Need Now

New York NPs under 3,600 hours of experience need a written practice agreement and written protocols with a collaborating physician. At 3,600 hours, independent practice is permitted.

Reviewed by Philip Wasef, MD — actively collaborating physician, licensed in 45 states. Last updated: September 2026.

New York draws a clear line at 3,600 hours of experience. Below it, a written collaborative agreement and written practice protocols are required. Above it, independent practice is permitted and no agreement is needed. Which side of the threshold you're on determines whether you need a collaborating physician at all.

Quick Answers

Do New York NPs need a written collaborative agreement?
It depends on experience. Under 3,600 hours: yes — a written practice agreement and written protocols with a collaborating physician are both required. At 3,600 hours and beyond: no. NYSED permits independent practice past that threshold, with no agreement or protocols.
I have more than 3,600 hours of experience. Do I need a collaborating physician?
No. NYSED's rule is that NPs who have completed 3,600 hours may practice independently — no written agreement, no protocols required. Be aware of the sunset: this provision of NY Education Law § 6902 carries a statutory expiration currently set at July 1, 2030. It was previously set to expire July 1, 2026 and was extended; legislation to remove the sunset permanently is pending but has not passed. Confirm current status with the Office of the Professions before relying on it.
Can I start practicing right away?
Yes. New York requires no pre-approval. Once your agreement and practice protocol are signed you may begin immediately — unlike Pennsylvania, where you wait for a number to be issued.
How often must my physician review charts?
No less than every three months. NYSED requires peer review of patient records "in a timely fashion, but no less often than every 3 months." There's no mandatory percentage — the number of charts is left to the judgment of both parties, based on factors like your experience and practice setting.
How many NPs can one physician collaborate with?
Four, where the NPs practice at different locations. There is no cap on NPs practicing at the same location as the physician.
Can New York NPs prescribe Schedule II?
Yes. New York does not restrict NP Schedule II prescribing the way states like Ohio do, and there's no separate state DEA registration to obtain.
Do PAs need a collaborative practice agreement?
No. New York PAs don't require a collaborative practice agreement in the way NPs do.

The 3,600-Hour Threshold

New York takes a tiered approach to NP practice authority. Below 3,600 hours of experience, a written collaborative agreement and written practice protocols with a physician are required. At 3,600 hours, those requirements lift entirely.

The NYSED Office of the Professions states the rule as follows:

"Nurse practitioners, however, must practice in accordance with written practice protocols and a written practice agreement with a collaborating physician unless and until the NP has completed 3,600 hours of experience. Thereafter, the NP may practice independently."
— NYSED Office of the Professions, Practice Requirements for Nurse Practitioners

What this means practically: if you're below the threshold you need a collaborating physician, a signed agreement, and written protocols before seeing patients. If you've crossed it, none of that is required under state law.

Sunset notice: This provision of NY Education Law § 6902 currently carries a statutory expiration of July 1, 2030. The sunset has been extended before — it was previously set to expire July 1, 2026. Legislation to remove it permanently is pending but has not passed. NPs relying on the 3,600-hour threshold should confirm current status with the NYSED Office of the Professions.

The New York Process, Step by Step

  1. Draft and sign the written Collaborative Practice Agreement with your collaborating physician.
  2. Select an approved practice protocol. New York requires NPs to practice pursuant to written protocols reflecting the specialty area in which you're certified. NYSED maintains a list of approved protocol texts — choose from it rather than writing your own.
  3. Begin practicing. No pre-approval, no waiting for a number. Once the agreement and protocol are in place you can work.
  4. File Form 4NP within 90 days. Both you and your physician complete and sign it, and it goes to the NYSED Office of the Professions within 90 days of your first day of professional practice. This is a one-time submission, not an annual filing.
  5. Keep a copy at every practice setting, available for NYSED inspection.
  6. Your physician reviews charts no less than every three months — and documents it.

Mistakes I Actually See in New York Arrangements

Not knowing where you stand on the 3,600-hour threshold.

NYSED doesn't send a notice when you cross it. NPs who've hit 3,600 hours sometimes continue paying for a collaboration arrangement they no longer need. NPs who are short of it sometimes assume they've crossed because the number is rarely stated plainly. Know your count.

Missing the 90-day Form 4NP window.

You can practice immediately, which is genuinely convenient — and it's exactly why the filing gets forgotten. There's no gate to remind you.

Chart review that drifts past three months.

The rule is "no less often than every 3 months," and it's the physician's obligation. Quarterly slips to twice a year without anyone deciding to let it. Documentation matters as much as the review.

Assuming the four-NP cap applies to your situation when it doesn't — or the reverse.

The cap is on NPs at different locations. If you practice at the same location as your collaborating physician, it doesn't bind. Worth clarifying before you assume a physician is unavailable.

One copy instead of one per setting.

The agreement must be available at every practice setting for NYSED inspection, not filed centrally.
